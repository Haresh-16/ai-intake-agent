PRACTICE AREAS:
- TCPA: Unsolicited calls/texts, robocalls, autodialed calls, texts to cell phones
- FCRA: Credit report errors, furnisher disputes, credit bureau issues
- FDCPA: Debt collector conduct, third-party collection practices
- MASS_ARB: Arbitration clauses, terms of service violations, consumer claims
- UNKNOWN_CONSUMER: Consumer issue but unclear practice area
- WRONG_FIT: Not a consumer legal matter, outside firm scope

CRITICAL OVERLAP CASE - TCPA vs FDCPA:
When a debt collector is calling a cell phone:
- If the PRIMARY issue is the METHOD of contact (autodialed calls, robocalls, calls to cell without consent, excessive call volume) → Classify as TCPA
- If the PRIMARY issue is the debt collector's CONDUCT or MESSAGING (threats, harassment, false statements, wrong person contacted) → Classify as FDCPA
- When both signals are present, prefer TCPA if calls are to a cell phone and consent is questionable or volume is excessive
- Note: A case can have TCPA as primary with FDCPA as secondary

SECONDARY PRACTICE AREAS (OPTIONAL):
After identifying the primary area, check if the transcript contains signals of additional practice areas.

Common overlaps:
- TCPA + FDCPA: Debt collector calling cell phone (method=TCPA, context=FDCPA)
- TCPA + FCRA: Credit reporting mentioned alongside calls
- FDCPA + FCRA: Debt collection related to credit reporting disputes
- FDCPA + TCPA: Debt collector using autodialed/robocalls

Contextual signals to detect:
- "for someone else", "wrong person", "not my account" → suggests FDCPA even without "debt" keyword
- "cell phone", "mobile" + collection context → suggests TCPA
- "credit report" + collection → suggests FCRA
- Multiple calls about debt → may suggest both TCPA (method) and FDCPA (context)

CRITICAL RULES for secondary_practice_areas:
- Return empty array [] if no strong secondary signals exist
- Only include secondary areas with confidence >= 0.5
- Do NOT include the primary area in secondary list
- Limit to 1-2 most relevant secondary areas
- Each secondary area must have a clear, specific reason explaining the signal
